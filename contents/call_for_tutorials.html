Call for EDBT 2027 tutorials

We solicit proposals for tutorials for presentation at the EDBT 2027 conference to be held in Lille, France. Proposals must provide a well-structured survey of scientific advance selected topic (as opposed to a mere list of past results), with the option of describing the most significant, impactful and/or promising works in depth. Proposals should include a meaningful summary of open issues in the area. The topic of the tutorial should be broad enough to attract a significant audience and must include enough details to provide a sense of both the scope of the material to be covered, and the depth to which it will be covered.

Submission Guidelines

For the 2027 tutorial track, we encourage submissions of tutorials in one of the two following formats:

  • Short (4 pages proposals, including references): this is the format used in previous years. In 4 pages, authors can provide an outline of the material they plan to cover, but details and a developed description of the topic would not fit.
  • Long (12 pages surveys, plus unlimited space for references): we particularly encourage authors to write informative, well-structured, comprehensive surveys of a scientific area, on which the proposed tutorial will be based. We expect such surveys, companions to the tutorials, to serve as reference for future research in our community.

Both short and long tutorial submissions will be peer-reviewed by a dedicated committee, and the accepted ones will be included in the proceedings.

Tutorial submissions should be formatted according to the EDBT 2027 formatting guidelines available here.

The tutorial proposals should include their names and affiliations. Any submitted proposal fitting none of the two accepted categories (in length, file type, or formatting requirements) will be desk rejected. We suggest the following content in your tutorial proposal:

  • Title
  • Preferred duration (1.5 or 3 hours; if the tutorial can be of either length, please be sure to identify which material in each case)
  • Abstract
  • Outline presenting the scope and depth of the coverage of the topics to be addressed by the tutorial. Please associate the different parts with the name of the presenter.
  • Goals and objectives
  • Intended audience and any prerequisite knowledge the audience must have
  • Biography (no longer than a few sentences) of the speakers, including contact information. We expect at least one senior presenter to be included. Please also indicate the approximate length of time each presenter will be speaking.
  • If an earlier version of the tutorial has been presented in a different venue, indicate those venues as well as the difference in the content with the other editions of the tutorial.

Tutorials that bridge the gap between database research and other disciplines, and those showing the relevance of database technology in those disciplines are of particular interest.

The conference-wide guidelines concerning scientific misconduct will apply during evaluation.

Selected tutorials will be archived as articles in the conference proceedings. An article (4 pages) will be requested from the authors. It is expected to be different from the tutorial proposal. It should focus on providing an introduction to the presented topic, highlighting the main related work, summarizing the content of the tutorial, and be educational in nature. Details on the formatting of the tutorial articles will be provided to the authors of tutorials selected for presentation at EDBT 2027 at a later date.
