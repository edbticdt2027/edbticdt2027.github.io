"Graph Querying After GQL: Lessons from Standardization"

by Liat Peterfreund

School of Computer Science and Engineering, The Hebrew University of Jerusalem

Abstract: Graph query languages have evolved significantly in recent years, including the development of GQL, the first ISO-standardized query language for property graphs. The development and standardization of GQL and SQL/PGQ have made many design choices explicit, bringing the semantic, expressive, and computational consequences of these choices into focus. In this talk, I will share lessons from our formal study of these languages, together with insights from my experience in the standardization process. The talk will explore questions such as which objects should be first-class, what iteration should carry, and how different query models should interact. These design decisions shape what can be expressed directly, what must be encoded, and what information remains available for further querying and optimization. They also reveal an important interplay between language and data representation. That interplay becomes especially relevant as language models increasingly generate queries, implicitly making choices that were previously made by query authors and language designers. I will conclude by discussing what this shift may mean for the design, analysis, and validation of query languages.

Liat Peterfreund is an Assistant Professor at the School of Computer Science and Engineering at the Hebrew University of Jerusalem. Her research focuses on the foundations of data management, with an emphasis on query languages, their formal semantics, expressive power, and complexity. She is actively involved in the development and standardization of graph query languages, including GQL and SQL/PGQ, and works on bridging theoretical foundations with practical systems. Her work has been recognized by several awards, including a SIGMOD Research Highlights Award and the L'Oréal-UNESCO For Women in Science Award.

"Multiplying Circuits Is All You Need: From Decision Diagrams to Language Models"

by Guy Van den Broeck

University of California, Los Angeles

Abstract: Circuits are a common language for reasoning in databases and artificial intelligence: computation graphs that guarantee tractable inference for queries such as model counting and marginal probabilities. This talk is about what happens when we can multiply two circuits efficiently. First, in logical circuit compilation, multiplication builds circuits bottom-up. I will present tree decision diagrams (TDD), a new class of circuits that uses this multiplication operator to scale up compilation while keeping circuits canonical, reviving bottom-up compilation and outperforming existing bottom-up compilers by an enormous margin. Second, in language models, multiplication gives control. Multiplying a probabilistic circuit that approximates the distribution of the language model's future behavior with a circuit encoding a constraint or objective function gives state-of-the-art controllable text generation. This opens up interesting theoretical questions about which constraints can be represented compactly as circuits, and under which conditions such circuits can be multiplied efficiently.

Guy Van den Broeck is a Professor at UCLA, in the Computer Science Department, where he directs the StarAI lab. His research interests are in Machine Learning, Knowledge Representation and Reasoning, and Artificial Intelligence in general. He serves as program chair of ICLR 2027, and has served as (senior) area chair for NeurIPS, ICML, AISTATS, UAI, AAAI, and IJCAI. His papers have been recognized with awards from key conferences such as AAAI, UAI, KR, and OOPSLA. Guy is the recipient of an NSF CAREER award, a Sloan Fellowship, and the IJCAI-19 Computers and Thought Award.
